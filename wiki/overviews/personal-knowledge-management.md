# Personal Knowledge Management

> **Status**: skeletal stub. Will grow as additional PKM-adjacent sources (Zettelkasten, *Building a Second Brain*, Andy Matuschak's notes, *How to Take Smart Notes*, Niklas Luhmann, etc.) are ingested. Currently leans entirely on a single source — [[sources/llm-wiki-idea]].

## Working thesis

The dominant constraint in personal knowledge work is **not** reading or thinking — it is the **bookkeeping of cross-references, summaries, and contradictions** that makes a body of knowledge usable over time. Approaches that fail to solve the bookkeeping problem fail to compound, regardless of how good the ideas they capture are.[^src1]

## Sub-topics so far

- **The LLM Wiki pattern** — see [[llm-wiki-pattern]]. Argues that LLMs solve the bookkeeping problem that defeated [[memex]] and most human wikis.
- **RAG-first vs wiki-first knowledge tools** — see [[rag-vs-persistent-wiki]]. [[notebooklm]] is the canonical RAG-first example.
- **Intellectual history** — [[vannevar-bush]]'s 1945 [[memex]] essay is the named ancestor.

## Open questions (to investigate as more sources arrive)

- How does this pattern relate to **Zettelkasten** (Luhmann)? Both emphasize linking; Zettelkasten emphasizes atomic notes and emergent structure. Where do they agree/diverge?
- How does it relate to Tiago Forte's **Building a Second Brain** (PARA, CODE)? BASB is more capture/curation focused — does it offer anything the LLM Wiki pattern is missing?
- How does it relate to **Andy Matuschak's evergreen notes**? Matuschak's "notes should be densely linked" claim is consistent; his "notes should be concept-oriented" claim is too.
- Is there empirical data on the failure rates of human-maintained wikis vs LLM-assisted ones? The source's claim is intuitive but unverified.
